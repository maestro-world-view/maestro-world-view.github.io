        Applying for English-language Jobs in Berlin, Hamburg or Munich?

        As someone that has lived, worked and traveled across Germany for many years, I am always amused at how Expats and International jobseekers try to utilise the Lebenslauf Format (The Native German Resume Format) in order to gain employment in Berlin, Hamburg and across Germany. Worse still is the Europass format which was obviously created by somebody in the inner echelons of the confused and idea/allergic EU labyrinth. Stay away from both of these badly thought-out CV Writing formats, especially if your German language skills are not perfect. The best suggestion for a native English-language speaker is to be authentic, clear in your communication and honest about both your language skills and your knowledge of the German market. Use a 2-page format with an introduction, skills and notable achievements section. Presume at all times that the reader speaks excellent English, or in most cases that the HR Manager or Recruiter is actually a native English speaker themselves. This will almost certainly be true when targeting some of the many start-up scenes across Germany in Berlin, Munich, Hamburg and other major cities.

        AI-Powered Hiring in Germany - The new Way

        AI recruitment in Germany is becoming increasingly prominent as companies seek to optimize their hiring processes using advanced technologies. The integration of artificial intelligence (AI) into recruitment practices has been embraced across various industries, with the aim of enhancing efficiency, reducing biases, and improving candidate selection. Local and international solutions like Prescreen, Softgarden, Personio, and Truffls are widely used. Additionally, global platforms like HireVue and Manatal have made their mark in the German market, offering AI-powered features tailored to local needs. One of the primary motivations behind AI recruitment in Germany is the reduction of bias in hiring. With Germany’s strong emphasis on diversity and inclusion, AI tools are leveraged to minimize unconscious biases that can occur in traditional hiring processes. Germany is also known for its stringent data protection laws, particularly under the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR). AI recruitment tools used in the country must comply with these regulations, ensuring that candidate data is handled securely and transparently.
